Parameters

How the model works

  • Discrete event. The model keeps a list of future events (a truck arrives, a station finishes a rack, a shift ends, a machine breaks) and jumps from one to the next. Nothing happens in between.
  • Flow. Receiving, kitting, assembly, leak test (AI racks only), functional test, burn in, final inspection and pack, dispatch. A rack that fails leak test, functional test or burn in goes to repair and then repeats the test.
  • Finite buffers. A finished rack can only leave when the next buffer has a free slot. Until then its station is blocked. A station with nothing to work on is starved.
  • AMRs. Every move needs a robot from a shared fleet. A finished rack waiting for a robot still occupies its station.
  • Burn in. A rack starts only if a bay is free and the zone has enough spare power and, for AI racks, enough spare liquid cooling. These are hard limits.
  • Shifts. Manual stations work the staffed hours and pause in between. Test, burn in and AMRs run around the clock.
  • Phase 2. Extra bays and stations can switch on partway through the run.

Why distributions and not fixed times

  • Triangular for manual work (fastest, typical, slowest). Example: assembly is usually 3.5 hours, never under 2.5, sometimes 5. It is the honest choice when all you have is three estimates from the floor.
  • Lognormal for test and repair. Most finish near the average and a few run very long, like a repair that turns into a part swap.
  • Exponential for time between breakdowns. A breakdown is equally likely in any hour, whatever happened before.
  • With fixed times every line looks perfect on paper. Queues, blocking and starving only appear when times vary, and that is the whole reason to simulate.

Why warm up and replications

  • Warm up. The line starts empty, so the first days look better than normal. Those days are run but not counted.
  • Replications. One run is one roll of the dice. The same scenario is run several times with different random numbers, and results are shown as an average with the lowest and highest run.

What it does not model

  • Floor layout, aisle widths, travel paths or AMR traffic. Move times are a distribution, not a map.
  • AMR charging and AMR breakdowns.
  • Part shortages. Material always arrives complete.
  • Operators as a separate resource, skills, breaks, or learning curves.
  • Rack configurations beyond two types, changeovers, or engineering holds.
  • Scrap. Every rack is eventually repaired and shipped.
  • Cluster level (multi rack) testing, firmware loading as a separate step, or customer acceptance.
  • Cost, other than the value of work in progress.
